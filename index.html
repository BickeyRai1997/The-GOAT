1987 – 2026

Two decades, one shirt number

1987
Born June 24 in Rosario, Argentina. Starts playing for local club Grandoli, coached by his father.
2000
Moves to Spain to join FC Barcelona's famed youth academy, La Masia, after the club agrees to fund his growth-hormone treatment.
2004
Makes his first-team debut for Barcelona at 17, becoming the youngest player to appear for the club in a competitive match at the time.
2009
Wins his first Ballon d'Or after a treble-winning season with Barcelona, scoring in the Champions League final.
2012
Scores 91 goals across club and country in a single calendar year, a widely cited modern-era record.
2021
Finally lifts a major trophy with Argentina, winning the Copa América. Leaves Barcelona after 21 years and joins Paris Saint-Germain.
2022
Captains Argentina to the FIFA World Cup title in Qatar, capping the tournament with a Golden Ball–winning performance.
2023
Signs with Inter Miami CF, sparking a wave of interest in Major League Soccer, and wins a record eighth Ballon d'Or.
2024
Retains the Copa América with Argentina, becoming the tournament's most-capped player in the process and pushing his career trophy haul into rarefied territory.
2025
Leads Inter Miami to its first MLS Cup, adds the MLS Golden Boot, and becomes the first player in league history to win back-to-back MVP awards.
2026
At 39, plays a fifth World Cup and captains Argentina back to the final. Spain edge it 1-0 in extra time, and Messi leaves the field in tears after collecting his runner-up medal — becoming just the second man, after Cafu, to appear in three World Cup finals. A fitting, hard-fought last bow on football's biggest stage.
Why "the GOAT"

The case for the debate

Unmatched individual honors
No player has won more Ballon d'Or awards. Messi's eight wins stand alone in the trophy's history.
The missing piece, found
Critics once pointed to an empty international cabinet. The 2021 Copa América and 2022 World Cup closed that argument for good.
A style all his own
Low center of gravity, a left foot that never seems rushed, and a habit of making the decisive play look inevitable in hindsight.
